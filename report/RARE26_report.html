RARE26 — Barrett's Neoplasia Detection

Methods, measurements and outcomes · state as of 18 August 2026

1. Summary

The task is image-level detection of neoplasia in Barrett's oesophagus, scored on PPV@90%Recall at a simulated 100:1 negative:positive prevalence. Because recall is pinned at 0.90, the score reduces to a single order statistic: PPV = 0.90 / (0.90 + 100·FPR@90R), where the threshold is set by the bottom-decile positive. Chance is 0.0099.

Three submissions were made. The final system is a two-arm ensemble of a domain-pretrained DINOv2 ViT-B/14 with LoRA adapters and a GastroNet+SWSL ResNet50 with restricted adaptation, combined by frozen per-model Platt calibration and equal-weight probability averaging.

Headline outcome. Two interventions produced measurable gains across roughly fourteen tested: replacing the ImageNet-pretrained backbone with an endoscopy-pretrained one (AUPRC 0.634 → 0.873 and 0.863 → 0.975 on held-out centres), and adding a second, architecturally distinct arm. Everything else measured null or harmful.

2. What was used

2.1 Data

The challenge training release only: 3,195 images, 208 positives (6.5%), three centres. One centre (evc, 100 images) is degenerate — 50% prevalence and ~1.0 AUROC without training — and is excluded from all headline metrics, leaving two usable evaluation centres. Per-centre prevalence is 2.68% (center_1, n=2,279) and 11.89% (center_2, n=816). No patient, video, examination, imaging-mode or FOV-geometry metadata is populated. Native resolution is approximately 640×512.

No external data was used. The rules permit public datasets, but this challenge is the direct sequel to RARE25 and the hidden test set is drawn from the same twelve-centre consortium, so Barrett's images taken from affiliated publications carry a concrete risk of overlap with the hidden test set.

Two combination decisions were forced by measurement, not preference. Fitted Platt slopes span 0.46–1.19 and intercepts −3.25 to +1.98 across the ten checkpoints, so the arms are not on a common scale; the uncalibrated probability average measured negative on both centres (center_2 AUPRC −0.0187, P(gain) 0.039). Weights are equal by predeclaration because, with two usable centres, any fitted weight is selected on the same data that would validate it.

3. Validation protocol

All comparisons use leave-one-centre-out: train on the other centres, test on a centre no model has seen, with a paired patient-clustered bootstrap over 2,000 draws. This replaced pooled cross-validation, which produced four separate wrong conclusions — it argued for keeping a harmful ResNet fusion, hid a centre-prevalence shortcut, gave a noise-dominated PPV estimate, and most recently called positive-enriched batching a 39% FPR improvement that leave-one-centre-out scored at zero.

The protocol's direction is validated; its magnitudes are not. A change it called large and positive moved the leaderboard rank from 103 to 62. But local FPR@90R is 0.6–3.6% against a leaderboard 58.7% — optimism of 17–100×. No local number should be read as an expected leaderboard number.

The second result identifies what kind of ensembling pays. The retired three-recipe ensemble shared one backbone: members correlated at Spearman 0.69–0.91 and agreed on ~75% of the bottom-decile positives. It cut in-distribution FPR@90R by 39% and moved the leaderboard by 1.2 points. The two-arm fusion's members correlate at 0.48–0.59 and share only ~50% of those threshold-setting positives. Diversity of representation, not of training recipe, is what transfers.

A related finding: frozen-probe screening does not predict adapted performance. The screen ranked SWSL below plain GastroNet-5M (0.659/0.918 vs 0.686/0.933); adapted, SWSL clearly won (0.859/0.967 vs 0.805/0.944). The screen then ranked plain GastroNet the best third arm; adapted, it dilutes.

4.3 Two hypotheses refuted by measurement

  • Acquisition/device shift. Models scored under 36 graded corruptions (gamma 0.45–2.10, white balance ±22%, brightness, contrast, sharpness, JPEG q25–60, blur, downsampling to 25%, noise, vignette). Worst FPR@90R across all 36: 1.67% and 4.82%; AUPRC never fell below 0.83 and several corruptions improved it. Matching the leaderboard would need ~17×; the worst corruption manages 1.35×.
  • Cross-centre score-scale drift. Pooled AUROC 0.9819 against a mean within-centre 0.9777 — pooling helps. A 90%-recall threshold calibrated on one centre yields FPR 0.0691 on its own negatives and 0.0686 on the other centre's: an inflation factor of 0.99×.

5. Diagnosis

The leaderboard AUROC of 0.820 against 0.98 locally shows the whole ranking degrading on unseen centres, not merely the positive tail. Two independent adversarial reviews converged on the same quantitative account.

If a fraction f of hidden positives are frames in which no lesion is reliably visible — exam-level labelling, frame selection, post-treatment cases — then forcing 90% recall requires reaching into that pool, and FPR@90R = (f − 0.1) / f.

Our 58.7% implies f ≈ 0.24. First place's 26.0% implies f ≈ 0.135. The same mixture reproduces the observed AUROC: 0.98·(1−f) + 0.50·f ≈ 0.82.

One model therefore accounts for AUROC 0.820, AUPRC 0.343, FPR@90R 58.7%, and for why fourteen levers returned null: none of them made a previously invisible lesion visible. On this reading the distance between rank 72 and rank 1 is finding the lesion in roughly 10% more of the hard positives — a detection-sensitivity problem, not a calibration or ensembling one.

The metric's sensitivity makes the scale concrete: dPPV/dFPR ≈ −0.0253, so one point of FPR is worth +0.00025 PPV. Reaching the top ten requires roughly twenty points. The fifteen-model ensemble delivered 1.2.

6. Limitations

  • Two-point validation. With evc unusable, every comparison rests on two Dutch centres, reused across dozens of predeclared tests. This is closer to a development set than to external validation, and its effective validity is largely spent.
  • Pretraining overlap is not excluded. GastroNet covers eight Dutch hospitals and the challenge centres belong to the same consortium. A centre-identifiability probe was inconclusive: stock DINOv2 also fingerprints centres at 99.4%, so near-perfect recovery indicates acquisition differences rather than contamination.
  • Local magnitudes are not predictive. Demonstrated repeatedly, at 17–100×.
  • No lesion-level supervision exists in the data, so the leading diagnosis cannot be tested directly with what is available.

7. Reproduction

Training, evaluation and packaging are driven by the scripts in training/, evaluation/ and submission_template/. The standard comparison is:

python training/train_cross_centre.py --folds folds_v2.csv --arch dinov2 --holdout <centre> --init dinov2.pth --tag <name>
python evaluation/compare_loco.py --baseline runs/<base> --candidate runs/<cand> --folds folds_v2.csv

All flags default to previous behaviour so historical runs remain reproducible. Model loaders assert matched key counts, since strict=False silently yields a randomly initialised backbone. Every prediction CSV, log.csv and results.json for all runs is retained, so no conclusion in this report has lost its underlying evidence.
