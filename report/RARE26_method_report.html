6. Short description and motivation 134 / 150 words

Barrett’s neoplasia against non-dysplastic Barrett’s is a low-prevalence, low-contrast discrimination in which ensemble members sharing a visual representation fail on the same images. Our previous submission ensembled fifteen checkpoints over one backbone with three training recipes: it cut in-distribution FPR@90R by 39% but moved the leaderboard 1.2 points, because recipe diversity does not decorrelate errors.

This system fuses two arms differing in pretraining corpus and architecture rather than recipe — a GastroNet-5M self-supervised DINOv2 ViT-B/14 adapted by LoRA, and a Billion-Scale-SWSL + GastroNet-5M ResNet50 adapted at layer4. Their predictions correlate at Spearman 0.48–0.59 and share only about half the bottom-decile positives, the images that set the 90%-recall threshold. Each checkpoint is Platt-calibrated on its own out-of-fold predictions before averaging, because the two architectures produce incomparable logit scales: averaging raw scores measured negative on both held-out centres.

7. Implementation details

a. Model Architecture

Ten checkpoints in two arms of five folds each. No test-time augmentation.

Fusion. Each checkpoint’s logit passes through a frozen affine (Platt) calibration fitted on that checkpoint’s own out-of-fold predictions, then a sigmoid; probabilities are averaged within each arm, then the two arm means are averaged with equal weight. No noisy-OR, no prior shift, no learned or fitted fusion weights, no rank normalisation. Weights are equal by predeclaration: with two usable centres, any fitted weight would be selected on the data that would validate it.

b. Additional data

Yes — for self-supervised pretraining, plus 100 labelled images in the training pool.

  • GastroNet-5M (~4.8M endoscopy images, 8 Dutch hospitals, 2012–2020), obtained from Theta Vision Cortex. Used as the self-supervised initialisation of both arms. Publicly available; not redistributed.
  • Billion-Scale SWSL (semi-weakly supervised, ImageNet-scale), combined with GastroNet-5M in the published ResNet50 checkpoint.
  • EndoVis 2015 Barrett’s set (evc): 100 labelled images (50 neoplasia / 50 non-dysplastic), 39 patients, included in the five-fold training pool and excluded from all reported evaluation metrics.
  • Challenge data: RARE training release, 3,095 images (center_1: 2,279 images, 61 neoplasia; center_2: 816 images, 97 neoplasia).
  • Total training pool: 3,195 images, 208 neoplasia.
  • EDD2020 was used only in a segmentation experiment that measured negative and forms no part of this system. No private or non-public data was used at any point.

c. Generative approach

No.

d. Supervised approach

Yes. Binary image-level classification with BCEWithLogitsLoss and a global pos_weight for class imbalance (centre-conditional balancing was tested and rejected). Five-fold grouped cross-validation; within each fold’s training portion an inner 1/5 split supplies the early-stopping signal. Checkpoint selection on validation AUPRC, patience 7, maximum 30 epochs. Mixed-precision (fp16 autocast) training.

e. Data (pre and post) processing

  • Pre-processing (P0): direct resize to the arm’s input size, then ImageNet mean/std normalisation — nothing else. Two alternatives were tested on the shipping system and rejected: masking the dark endoscope border (P3; center_1 AUPRC −0.0210, CI excluding zero) and per-image grey-world white balance (P4).
  • Training augmentation: none. Acquisition-style augmentation, jigsaw tile shuffling and the RARE25 winner’s heavy augmentation floor were each measured; none survived as a lever for the shipped arms.
  • Post-processing: per-checkpoint Platt affine calibration frozen at container build time, then equal-weight probability averaging. Percentile-rank fusion was deliberately avoided: ranks would be computed within whatever stack the container is handed, while the metric is pooled over the entire test set, so per-stack normalisation would make every prediction depend on its batch’s composition.

f. Input resolution

Arm A 336×336 (336/14 = a 24×24 patch grid, the checkpoint’s native grid); 224 and 448 both measured worse. Arm B 384×384.

8. Train / validation splitting

  • Five-fold cross-validation over the full 3,195-image pool, grouped by group_id (3,117 groups, at most 8 images per group) so no patient/lesion group spans a fold boundary. Folds hold 638–640 images with 41–42 neoplasia each.
  • Within each fold’s training portion, an inner 1/5 split provides the validation signal for early stopping and checkpoint selection (AUPRC, patience 7).
  • Each of the ten checkpoints is Platt-calibrated on its own out-of-fold predictions — data that checkpoint never trained on.
  • Design decisions were not made on this cross-validation. Every architecture and ablation decision was judged by leave-one-centre-out (train on the remaining centres, evaluate on the held-out centre) with a paired, patient-clustered bootstrap. Pooled and per-fold CV misled us repeatedly — it concealed a centre-prevalence shortcut and produced a cross-fold score-scale artifact that does not exist at deployment.

9. Short description of results 162 / 200 words

The predeclared leave-one-centre-out gate compared the two-arm fusion against the single-arm baseline (LoRA-adapted GastroNet DINOv2). On center_1, AUPRC rose 0.8710 → 0.9012 (+0.0290, P(gain) 0.945) and FPR@90R fell 0.0356 → 0.0140. On center_2, AUPRC 0.9735 → 0.9754 (+0.0018, P(gain) 0.703) and FPR@90R 0.0042 → 0.0014. All six deltas across both centres were positive — no previous ensemble change achieved that.

On the Open Development leaderboard, these same ten checkpoints carrying an additional prior-shift and noisy-OR pooling stage scored PPV@90R 0.0150 [0.0108, 0.0746], AUROC 0.8195, AUPRC 0.3187; on the RARE25 validation block, PPV@90R 0.0155, AUROC 0.8889, AUPRC 0.5317.

That pooling stage has been removed from the submitted container: externally it cost 0.0239 AUPRC against our preceding submission and delivered none of the tail gain it was intended to buy. The submitted system is plain per-model Platt calibration and equal-weight probability averaging — the exact configuration that passed the gate above, before any tail machinery was layered on it. This configuration has not itself been scored on the development leaderboard.

10. Potential drawbacks 179 / 200 words

Local validation cannot see the failure mode. Leave-one-centre-out runs at AUROC 0.98–0.99 while the development leaderboard runs at 0.82, and a 36-condition acquisition-shift stress sweep bottoms out at 0.9669. No local perturbation available to us reaches within 0.15 AUROC of the scored regime, so most levers we tested are untested rather than refuted.

The development signal is not decidable. At roughly 103 development positives, PPV@90R is set by the 10th-percentile positive — a single image. Two poolings correlating at Spearman 0.99997 differed 2.5-fold in FPR@90R.

Possible pretraining-corpus overlap. Both arms initialise from GastroNet, self-supervised on 8 Dutch hospitals from the same consortium as the challenge centres. A centre-identifiability probe was inconclusive — stock DINOv2 also fingerprints centres at 99.4% — so we cannot exclude that some of our advantage is corpus familiarity rather than generalisation, and performance may degrade on centres outside that corpus.

Other limits. Fusion weights are equal by predeclaration, forgoing any genuine gain from weighting. The system outputs no spatial localisation; a segmentation route was built and measured negative. Folds are grouped by group_id, not by verified patient identity.

12. Optional: feedback for a next iteration of RARE

  • The development metric is not usable for model selection at its current size. With ~103 positives, PPV@90R is determined by a single image and its confidence interval spans roughly sixfold. We measured two poolings correlating at Spearman 0.99997 that differed 2.5-fold on it. Reporting a supplementary average-discrimination metric prominently, or enlarging the development positive count, would let teams separate real improvements from order-statistic noise.
  • The secondary “Validation RARE25” metric block is valuable but undocumented. It appeared on our evaluation page without explanation; describing it in the phase documentation would make it usable.
  • The unlabeled release is the most distinctive resource in RARE26 and, per the RARE25 organizers, no team attempted anomaly or one-class detection. A short baseline, or explicit encouragement, might unlock that direction.
